1

Immediate

ABCs, lying flat, IV access, continuous cardiac monitoring, 12-lead ECG, glucose

2

Risk Stratify

Apply Canadian Syncope Risk Score. Admit all high-risk patients (score ≥0) for monitored bed. Intermediate risk: ED observation, repeat ECG, serial troponin.

3

Cardiogenic syncope

Urgent cardiology review. Atropine 500mcg IV for symptomatic bradycardia. Transcutaneous pacing if no response. Treat specific arrhythmia per ALS algorithm.

4

Orthostatic hypotension

IV fluids if dehydrated. Review medications. Compression stockings. Tilt training. Consider fludrocortisone via GP.

5

Vasovagal (low-risk)

Reassurance. Avoid triggers. Physical counterpressure manoeuvres. Increase fluid/salt intake. Discharge with GP follow-up. No driving until cleared.

🚗 DVLA: All syncope patients must be advised not to drive until cause established. Group 1 (car/motorcycle): notify if recurrent/unexplained. Group 2 (LGV/PSV): notify immediately. Document advice given.

1

Immediate — Universal Antidotes

  • Glucose 10% IV 100ml if CBG <4mmol/L (or CBG not available)
  • Thiamine 500mg IV over 30min if alcohol use disorder / malnourished / any unexplained AMS (before glucose if Wernicke's suspected)
  • Naloxone 400mcg IV/IM/IN titrate if opioid toxidrome (miosis + reduced GCS + bradypnoea)
  • Oxygen to target SpO2 94-98%
2

Treat Underlying Cause

  • Meningitis: Ceftriaxone 2g IV immediately + Dexamethasone 10mg IV (do not delay for CT/LP)
  • Sepsis: cultures, broad-spectrum antibiotics (per sepsis protocol)
  • DKA/HHS: insulin + fluids (see DKA protocol)
  • Hypercalcaemia: IV fluids, bisphosphonate
  • Hepatic encephalopathy: lactulose, treat precipitant, rifaximin
3

Delirium Management (non-pharmacological first)

  • Quiet, well-lit environment; reorientation; familiar faces
  • Ensure glasses/hearing aids in situ
  • Avoid restraints; 1:1 nursing if needed
  • Correct sleep-wake cycle
4

Sedation if Risk to Self/Others (last resort)

Haloperidol 0.5–2mg PO/IM (avoid in Parkinson's/DLB/alcohol withdrawal/QTc prolongation)
Lorazepam 0.5–1mg IV/IM — preferred in alcohol withdrawal, seizures, NMS
Olanzapine 5–10mg IM — avoid combining with benzodiazepines IM

⚠️ NEVER give glucose without thiamine first in suspected Wernicke's — precipitates acute Korsakoff's

Benign Headache / Migraine Regimen

  • Paracetamol 1g PO
  • Ibuprofen 400-600mg PO (if not contraindicated)
  • Metoclopramide 10mg IV/PO (anti-dopaminergic — also treats nausea)
  • Prochlorperazine 12.5mg IM / 3mg buccal — alternative to metoclopramide
  • IV fluids if dehydrated
  • Dexamethasone 8-12mg IV/PO — reduces migraine recurrence at 24-48h (NNT ~5)
2

Migraine-specific

  • Sumatriptan 50-100mg PO / 6mg SC / nasal spray — if not given ergotamine
  • Dark, quiet room; rest
3

Cluster Headache

  • 100% O₂ via NRB mask 12-15L/min for 15-20 min (first-line)
  • Sumatriptan 6mg SC (most effective abortive)
  • Refer to neurology / headache clinic for prophylaxis (verapamil)
4

Suspected SAH

Urgent non-contrast CT head → LP if CT negative and >6h from onset → Neurosurgery referral if positive

5

Suspected Bacterial Meningitis

DO NOT DELAY antibiotics for LP/CT. Ceftriaxone 2g IV immediately + Dexamethasone 10mg IV

6

Temporal Arteritis (GCA)

Prednisolone 60mg PO immediately (do not wait for biopsy). Urgent ophthalmology if visual symptoms. ESR, CRP, temporal artery biopsy within 1 week.

Primary Survey + C-spine

2

Concussion (GCS 15, normal CT)

  • 24h period cognitive rest; avoid alcohol and contact sport
  • Discharge to responsible adult with written head injury advice
  • Return if: worsening headache, repeated vomiting, seizure, confusion, weakness, drowsiness
  • Graduated return-to-play/work protocol via GP
3

Raised ICP

  • Head of bed 30°
  • Mannitol 0.25–1g/kg IV over 15-20 min — discuss with neurosurgery
  • Or Hypertonic saline (NaCl 3%) 250ml IV
  • Controlled ventilation if intubated (target PaCO₂ 4.5-5kPa; hyperventilate to 4–4.5 only as temporising for acute herniation)
4

  • DOACs — Dabigatran: Idarucizumab (Praxbind) 5g IV
  • Factor Xa inhibitors: Andexanet alfa (discuss with haematology)
5

Neurosurgical Referral

Any significant intracranial haemorrhage, deteriorating GCS, mass effect/midline shift, compound depressed skull fracture, suspected penetrating injury

  • BP target before thrombolysis: <185/110mmHg. Use Labetalol 10-20mg IV to lower if needed
  • Post-thrombolysis: BP <180/105 for 24h, no antiplatelets for 24h, no NGT/catheter for 30 min
  • Monitor neurologically every 15 min for 2h
3

Thrombectomy (if eligible — NICE TA524)

  • Within 6h of onset (or up to 24h in selected wake-up/late-window patients)
  • Refer immediately to thrombectomy centre
  • Can be given alongside thrombolysis (bridging)
4

Antiplatelet Therapy (ischaemic stroke/TIA)

  • If thrombolysis given: start Aspirin 300mg OD after 24h (CT repeat to exclude haemorrhagic transformation first)
  • If no thrombolysis: Aspirin 300mg PO immediately
  • Dual antiplatelet (DAPT) for minor stroke/TIA: Aspirin 300mg + Clopidogrel 300mg stat then 75mg OD + Aspirin 75mg OD × 21 days (POINT trial — NICE supported)
5

BP Management

  • Do NOT lower BP acutely in ischaemic stroke (unless thrombolysis or SBP >220)
  • Permissive hypertension — allows collateral flow
  • Haemorrhagic stroke: target SBP <140 within 1h (use Labetalol IV)
6

TIA — ABCD² Score + Urgent Management

  • ABCD² ≥4 or AF suspected or crescendo TIA → admit / urgent assessment within 24h
  • Start Aspirin 300mg immediately
  • DAPT as above if minor stroke/TIA
  • MRI brain, Carotid Doppler, 24h ECG (AF?), echo (if cardioembolic source suspected)
  • Carotid endarterectomy within 2 weeks if 50-99% symptomatic stenosis

  • CIWA-Ar guided: treat if score >8
  • Chlordiazepoxide PO (preferred — long-acting, less addiction potential):
    CIWA 8-15: 25-50mg q2-4h prn | CIWA >15: 50-100mg q1-2h prn
    Typical reducing regime: 30mg QDS day 1 → taper over 5-7 days
  • Hepatic failure: Lorazepam 1-2mg PO/IV (safer than chlordiazepoxide as no active metabolites)
  • IV route if unable to take PO or severe withdrawal
3

Delirium Tremens (CIWA >20)

  • ICU/HDU — high mortality if untreated (5-15%)
  • Diazepam 10mg IV q5-10 min until calm (symptom-controlled — some patients need 100mg+)
  • Or Lorazepam 2-4mg IV q15 min
  • IV thiamine, IV fluids, electrolyte replacement (K, Mg, PO4)
  • Haloperidol for refractory hallucinations (adjunct only — not primary treatment)
4

Alcohol Withdrawal Seizures

  • Lorazepam 2-4mg IV — first-line (benzodiazepine, also treats withdrawal)
  • Adequate benzodiazepine dosing prevents further seizures
  • Phenytoin NOT recommended (ineffective for alcohol withdrawal seizures)
  • Ensure adequate thiamine replacement

Thyroid Storm — Treatment Sequence

  • Step 1 — Block synthesis: Propylthiouracil (PTU) 500-1000mg PO/NG stat, then 250mg 4-hourly (preferred over carbimazole in storm — also blocks T4→T3 conversion)

  • Passive rewarming — avoid active external rewarming (causes peripheral vasodilation + shock)
  • Correct hyponatraemia slowly. Treat hypoglycaemia. IV fluids cautiously (risk CCF).
  • ICU. Endocrinology referral urgently.

Epiglottitis

  • Sit patient upright. Do NOT lay down. Do NOT examine throat without airway team present.
  • Humidified O₂. Alert anaesthetics + ENT immediately.
  • IV access (calmly — anxiety worsens obstruction). Dexamethasone 8mg IV.
  • Ceftriaxone 2g IV (covers H. influenzae and Streptococcal)
  • Definitive airway (intubation or surgical) in controlled setting — theatre if possible
2

Quinsy (Peritonsillar Abscess)

  • ENT referral for aspiration or incision & drainage
  • Benzylpenicillin 1.2g IV QDS + Metronidazole 500mg IV TDS
  • Dexamethasone 8-10mg IV (reduces oedema and pain)
  • Adequate analgesia. IV fluids if unable to swallow.
  • Quinsy tonsillectomy: 6 weeks after resolution
3

Epistaxis

  • First aid: lean forward, pinch soft part of nose (not bridge) continuously for 15-20 min, mouth breathe
  • Examine: good light + Thudichum speculum after suction to identify bleeding point
  • Anterior bleed (Little's area):
    Topical: Lidocaine + adrenaline spray then silver nitrate cautery (one side only per episode)
    If uncontrolled: Nasopore/Rapid Rhino anterior nasal pack
  • Posterior bleed / anterior pack fails:
    ENT referral. Posterior balloon pack (Foley catheter 12-14Fr). Consider surgery/embolisation.
  • Anticoagulation: reverse only if life-threatening — discuss with haematology
  • Discharge: saline nasal spray, avoid nose blowing, no strenuous activity 2 weeks. Return if rebleeds.
4

Ludwig's Angina (Floor-of-mouth cellulitis)

  • Airway emergency — senior anaesthetics + ENT immediately
  • Co-amoxiclav 1.2g IV TDS + Metronidazole 500mg IV TDS
  • Early surgical drainage. ICU. Awake fibreoptic intubation if airway compromised.
5

Acute Mastoiditis

  • CT temporal bones (confirms diagnosis, identifies complications)
  • Ceftriaxone 2g IV BD — empirical
  • ENT urgently — may need myringotomy ± cortical mastoidectomy
  • Complications: subperiosteal abscess, meningitis, sinus thrombosis — CT head also
